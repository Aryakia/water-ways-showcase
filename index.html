Research problem

Affiliation is not research geography.

Researcher maps often place people at their universities. For water research that can be misleading: the system of interest may be a basin or aquifer hundreds or thousands of kilometres away.

Water Ways separates researcher identity, studied water systems, and institutional affiliation so each field represents a different type of evidence.

Current scope

From literature to geographic discovery.

The working catalogue includes 27 publication-linked water systems, 102 preserved researcher profiles, 303 source-derived collaboration links, 68 profiles with source-named studied systems, and 40 profiles currently matched to at least one 2025 transboundary-aquifer polygon.

Accessible System Dynamics Society proceedings from 1984–2026 and peer-reviewed System Dynamics Review records are part of the literature-discovery process.

What I built

Evidence-aware research infrastructure

Researcher + publication layer

Profiles, affiliations, methods, studies, and source links remain distinguishable rather than flattened into a single record.

Basin & aquifer geography

HydroBASINS v1c level 3 and IGRAC–UNESCO 2025 provide global spatial context.

Conservative matching

Uncertain or unmatched study-area names stay visible instead of being forced into unsupported polygons.

Basin-first discovery

Select a water system and move directly to associated studies and researchers.

Interactive exploration

Search, scope filters, independent layers, deep zoom, pan controls, and external profile/publication links.

Reusable research records

Filtered research-geography records can be exported for analysis and community development.

Evidence standard

Do not invent geography.

Peer-reviewed articles, conference records, and discovery profiles remain visibly distinct. A basin-study link requires a defensible named study area. A source-named system and a current GIS polygon match are treated as different evidence states.
